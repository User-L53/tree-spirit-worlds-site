The Internal Clock

An illustrated literary journey through childhood, memory, time, and our attempts to distance ourselves from a past that continues to shape us.

In a world where thoughts become places, five-year-old Sophie has all the time in the world.

She lives among ancient trees, rivers, and mountains with Rooty—the strange, gentle psyche of the world around her. Here, anger can become a mountain. A thought can change the weather. And time, Rooty tells her, does not move the way it does elsewhere.

But there is something Sophie cannot understand.

The woman she sometimes sees in the water keeps getting older. Sophie does not.

As the world around her begins to reveal what it has been holding, Sophie is drawn toward a question she has carried since she was very small: if everything changes, does that mean everything we love must eventually disappear?

The Internal Clock is an illustrated literary journey through childhood, memory, time, and our attempts to distance ourselves from a past that continues to shape us.

Set in a quiet, dreamlike world where consciousness takes physical form, it is a story about living with what has happened, letting go of what cannot be held, and finding a way to keep moving.
